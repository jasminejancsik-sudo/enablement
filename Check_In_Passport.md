## Welcome to Checkout.com
Welcome to the team. Over the next five weeks you will learn how payments work, what Checkout.com does for merchants and how to do the BDR role well. You don't need any payments experience to start. We'll build it with you, step by step.

Each week is named after a stage of a payment, and each week builds on the last. At the end of Week 5 you will take three certifications. When you pass them and every stamp in this Passport is signed off, you **Check Out**: certified and ready to start prospecting.

## Your certifications
You'll receive the full rubrics in Week 2. The pass mark is 80% on each rubric. If you don't pass the first time, you get coaching and a retake on Thursday of Week 5.

| Certification | What you'll do | What the assessors look for |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Social selling** | Submit your portfolio, then walk the assessors through it for 10 minutes | A professional profile, a researched outreach sequence for three target contacts and one published post |
| **2. Cold calling** | A 20-minute live role play with a buyer persona | A strong opening, earning permission to talk, discovery using DEPTH, handling objections and booking the meeting |
| **3. Pitch deck** | A 10-minute pitch and 5-minute Q&A for a panel | A clear link between the merchant's problem and our value, a story built for one industry and confident answers |

- Explain how a payment works and what Checkout.com does, in plain language

- Use LinkedIn to research, connect and stay visible to prospects
- Hand every meeting to your AE with a complete handoff note
- Keep Salesforce up to date the same day

- **Handoffs:** every meeting goes to your AE with a completed handoff note

## Payments glossary: starter terms
Add at least 20 terms of your own by the end of Week 2.

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Merchant** | A business that sells goods or services and accepts payments from its customers. Our customers are merchants. |
| **Cardholder** | The person paying with a card. |
| **Issuer** | The bank that gave the cardholder their card. It approves or declines each payment. |
| **Acquirer** | The bank or licensed company that processes card payments for the merchant and pays the money out to them. |
| **Card scheme** | The network that connects acquirers and issuers and sets the rules, such as Visa, Mastercard and American Express. |
| **Payment service provider (PSP)** | A company that lets merchants accept payments online, connecting them to acquirers and payment methods. |
| **Authorisation** | The step where the issuer approves or declines a payment. |
| **Capture** | The step where the merchant confirms it wants to collect an approved payment. |
| **Settlement** | The step where money moves from the issuer, through the acquirer, to the merchant. |
| **Acceptance rate** | The share of attempted payments that are approved. A higher rate means more revenue for the merchant. |
| **Decline** | A payment that the issuer refuses. |
| **Chargeback** | When a cardholder disputes a payment through their bank and the money is taken back from the merchant. |
| **Alternative payment method (APM)** | A way to pay other than a card, such as a digital wallet, a bank transfer or buy now, pay later. |
| **3D Secure (3DS)** | An extra check, such as a code or banking app approval, that confirms the cardholder is making an online payment. |
| **Tokenisation** | Replacing card details with a secure code (a token), so the real card number isn't stored or shared. |
| **Interchange fee** | A fee paid to the issuer on each card payment. |
| **Payout** | Sending money from a business to someone else, such as a marketplace paying its sellers. |
| **ICP (ideal customer profile)** | The type of merchant we target: the industries, sizes and needs that fit Checkout.com best. |
| **Multithreading** | Building relationships with several people at one target company, not just one contact. |

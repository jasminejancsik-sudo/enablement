# New York Sales Class: Onboarding Repository
*Source: [New York Sales Class – Onboarding Repository](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1cqKzHDs9KVDS616wynbUgFgrgOKayOayA8B7-XgqDqI/edit), owned by Francesco Falcone. Saved 2026-10-01 as input for sales onboarding plans.*

## What it is
A one-stop resource sheet for a new sales class, alongside the day-by-day plan. It has three tabs:
- **Knowledge Links:** about 65 curated resources, each with a short note on when to use it. Topics cover General/What Good Looks Like, Sales (books, LinkedIn prospecting), Payments (podcasts, books, internal academies, cheat sheets), and Checkout.com (call and outreach examples, account plans, tooling, collateral, fee analyses, pricing, reporting, verticals, underwriting docs, partner intel).
- **Who to Meet:** a prioritised P0–P3 list. Each person is marked *Individual* (reach out directly for coffee) or *Collective* (a group session gets organised).
- **Good Sales Nuggets:** short tips from the team: a post-demo email formula, a competitive defense playbook, listen/talk ratio, solution selling, and a LinkedIn Sales Navigator method.

## Worth carrying into future plans
- The notes column sets the order things should be learned in. Several resources say "will be covered in-depth at a later stage", "would cover later" or "Month 2 or 3". Fee analyses, the Payment Knowledge Share and Visa Direct are examples. This fits the feedback that heavy payments content shouldn't be front-loaded.
- The Individual/Collective split in "Who to Meet" is a good way to plan the meet-the-team block. Karlie's tracker uses a simpler version: name, role and a done checkbox.
- Flagged as out of date: the "Why Checkout Wins" battlecard and the Sales Tooling doc (pending the Jira migration). The MAF pdf is still "TO BE ADDED".
- Open comments in the sheet tag Jaime Yeager, Adam Friedman and Alaina Parisella on specific resources. An earlier comment tagged Artyom Gurevich and is now resolved.

### Good Sales Nuggets

- Topic — Notes
- Post-Demo Email Formula — The post-demo email formula that gets responses: 4 elements = guaranteed response<br>1. Thank you + key insight shared<br>2. bullet recap of agreed value<br>3. Specific next step + time already agreed on call.<br>4. Deck attached
- Competitive defense playbook — Never mention competitors first<br>If prospect raises them, say Great question!<br>Reframe around your key differentiators<br>Use "While others focus on X we believe Y"
- Focus on increasing Listen / Talk Ratio — Getting prospects to talk 90% of the time - by asking them things they care about using open ended questions. At the end they will learn to trust you more, simply because they spent time talking about things they spend the most time on.
- Solution Sellign (more on this in RevWay) — The key goal to achieve, ultimately, is to create an environment where it becomes a relationship that is joint ownership of the mission, and in my career, this is the most impact and buy-in apart from just discussing pricing.<br>This is important because you're getting the client to talk about the things they care the most, and often a side benefit is that they start to trust you a little quicker, because you're interested and you're getting them to talk about those issues.<br>Fundamentally, I would want to control the conversation and structure it in a way that guides the customer to a solution. Because for example, a 15% discount in payment fees is attractive to a lot of people, but to some it’s not as important as customer acquisition costs going down the drain due to payment failures.
- On Using LI Sales Navigator — my general method:<br>1. Check for Payments personas (bunch of payments titles)<br>2. Check for 1st, 2nd and team link connections (altogether) - check if anyone interesting on the first 5-10 results<br>AND then keyword search 'payments OR billing' >> start to manually review profiles<br>^^ while manually reviewing profiles, I'm looking for who is the connection and why the person is relevant (job history, current role or new to role)

## Part 1: Adult Learning Principles

### 1.1 Knowles' Andragogy: the six assumptions
Malcolm Knowles' model is the standard starting point for how adults learn. Each assumption below comes with what it means for sales enablement.

| # | Principle | What it means | What we do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Need to know** | Adults want to know *why* before they learn *what*. | Start every module with the link to revenue: "This is how you defend pricing against Adyen/Stripe in a CFO meeting." |
| 2 | **Self-concept** | Adults expect to direct their own learning and dislike being lectured. | Offer self-serve paths (LMS, Glean, Drive) with clear goals. Let experienced hires test out of content they already know. |
| 3 | **Experience** | Adults bring prior experience. It's useful to build on, and it can also carry habits that need unlearning. | Run diagnostics on day 1 so a 10-year payments AE and a career-switcher BDR don't get the same path. Use peer-led sessions and deal stories. |
| 4 | **Readiness** | Adults are most ready to learn something when they need it for their job right now. | Sequence content to match the job: prospecting before negotiation, discovery before commercial structuring. |
| 5 | **Problem-centred orientation** | Adults learn through solving problems, not through topics. | Build modules around situations ("merchant has low auth rates in LATAM") rather than products ("Module 4: Acquiring"). |
| 6 | **Internal motivation** | Adults are driven more by competence, status and autonomy than by compliance. | Use certifications people are proud of, leaderboards, manager recognition, and a visible link to commission. |

### 1.2 Learning science that makes training stick
| Principle | The evidence | How to apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **Forgetting curve** (Ebbinghaus) | Without reinforcement, most new information is forgotten within days to weeks. | A single training event isn't enough. Plan reinforcement for every key topic at day 2, week 1, week 3 and week 6. |
| **Spaced repetition** | Learning spread over time beats cramming for long-term retention. | Spread payments fundamentals across the first 6–8 weeks rather than a firehose week 1. Use short weekly quizzes. |
| **Retrieval practice** | Recalling information strengthens memory more than re-reading it. | Quiz, don't re-present. "Explain interchange++ to me like I'm a CFO" is better than re-reading the slide. |
| **Deliberate practice** | Skill grows through targeted practice with feedback, not through hours spent. | Role-play with scorecards (DEPTH), recorded pitch certifications, and focused feedback on one skill at a time. |
| **Cognitive load** | Working memory is limited, and overload stops learning. | Cut week 1 down to the minimum viable knowledge. Use 10–20 minute chunks. Leave edge cases until later. |
| **Interleaving** | Mixing related topics builds better judgment than practising one topic in a block. | Once basics are in place, mix objection types and verticals in role-play rather than drilling one at a time. |
| **Social learning** (Bandura) | People learn by watching others and copying them. | Have new hires shadow top performers, watch Gong call libraries, and write "how I won it" deal debriefs. |
| **Experiential cycle** (Kolb) | Experience → reflect → conceptualise → apply. | After each live call: a short reflection, a manager debrief, then try again. |

### 1.3 Frameworks to anchor design and measurement
- **70-20-10:** a rough guide, not a precise ratio. About 70% of learning happens on the job (live deals, stretch assignments), 20% through others (coaching, shadowing, peers) and 10% through formal training. **The point is that formal training alone won't ramp anyone. The 70 and the 20 have to be designed in too.**
- **Kirkpatrick's 4 levels:** (1) Reaction → (2) Learning → (3) Behaviour → (4) Results. Most enablement stops at level 1 or 2 (completion and quiz scores). The charter's measures (methodology adoption in Salesforce, win rate, time to first deal) sit at levels 3 and 4, which is where leaders pay attention.
- **Backward design:** start from the behaviour you want ("AE runs a DEPTH discovery that qualifies payment volume, current PSP and pain"), define how you'd observe it, and only then build content.

### 1.4 Design checklist (use it for every programme)
- [ ] Opens with *why it matters to your number*
- [ ] Built around a real scenario or problem, not a topic list
- [ ] Learners actively practise or recall at least every 10–15 min
- [ ] Includes a practice step with feedback (role-play, certification, live call)
- [ ] Has a reinforcement plan (spaced touches over 4–6 weeks)
- [ ] Gives the manager a role (coaching guide, observation scorecard)
- [ ] Has a measurable behaviour or outcome tied to it (Kirkpatrick L3/L4)
- [ ] Gives experienced hires a way to test out

## Part 2: The Best Way to Ramp a Commercial Team

### 2.1 Core principles
1. **Ramp by milestones, not calendar.** Define ramp as reaching performance milestones (first meeting, first qualified opp, first deal, % of quota). Time spent in onboarding doesn't count as ramp. Calendar targets (30/60/90) are the expected pace, not the definition.
2. **Front-load the minimum, then learn by doing.** Week 1 should cover only what a rep needs to have a credible first conversation. Get people into live (or realistic) customer situations early. That is the 70.
3. **Make ramp role-specific.** BDR, AE, AM and SE need different skills and hit different milestones. One shared core is fine; one shared path isn't.
4. **The manager matters most.** Research and practitioner data consistently show that frontline manager coaching affects ramp more than any content. Managers own the 30/60/90 plan. Enablement equips them to coach it.
5. **Certify, don't just complete.** Gate progression on shown ability (pitch certification, discovery role-play, product quiz ≥ 80%), not on finishing modules.
6. **Reinforce past day 90.** Ramp turns into everboarding with no gap. Spaced refreshers, monthly skills sessions and recertification keep the forgetting curve from undoing ramp.
7. **Measure leading indicators.** Activity and skill measures (calls, meetings, certification scores, DEPTH field completion) show ramp problems weeks before quota does.

### 2.2 Ramp architecture

```
 PRE-BOARDING        ONBOARD (Wk 1–2)       RAMP (Day 15–90)            EVERBOARD (90+)
 ───────────────     ──────────────────     ────────────────────────    ─────────────────
 Welcome pack        Checkout & NORAM       Role pathway + live deals   Monthly skills
 Tool access         Payments 101           Weekly manager 1:1 coaching Quarterly product
 Pre-reads (light)   RevWay/DEPTH intro     Shadow → co-lead → lead     Recertification
 Buddy assigned      Tools (SFDC, Gong,     Certifications at gates     Role transitions
 Manager 30/60/90    Glean, Drive)          Spaced reinforcement        (BDR→AE, new mgr)
                     First certification    Ramp review at 30/60/90
```

### 2.4 Roles and responsibilities during ramp
| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **New hire** | Owns their own progress, completes certifications, logs reflections |
| **Manager** | Owns the 30/60/90 plan, holds a weekly ramp 1:1, observes and scores calls, signs off gates |
| **Buddy / peer mentor** | Answers "how things actually work" questions, lets the new hire shadow, gives informal feedback |
| **Enablement** | Designs pathways, runs certifications, equips managers, tracks ramp data, spots people falling behind |
| **SE / Product / Deal Mgmt** | Run subject-matter sessions and join early deals |

### 2.5 Ramp metrics (tie to the charter's impact dashboard)
| Type | Metric |
|---|---|
| **Leading** | Time to first meeting · activity vs. target · certification pass rate and attempts · DEPTH fields completed in Salesforce · Gong talk ratio and discovery question count |
| **Lagging** | Time to first qualified opp · time to first closed deal · % of ramped quota attained at 3/6/9 months · pipeline generated in first 90 days |
| **Health** | New-hire NPS at 30/90 · 12-month retention · manager confidence rating · % of ramp milestones hit on time |

Compare each cohort to a baseline (current-state ramp time by role) so enablement impact is visible. Without a baseline, there's no way to show improvement.

### 2.6 Common pitfalls
- **Firehose week 1.** Five days of slides. Most of it is forgotten by week 3 (see the forgetting curve).
- **One-size-fits-all.** The same path for an industry veteran and a new grad.
- **Measuring completion, not capability.** 100% LMS completion with no change in behaviour.
- **Manager is not involved.** Enablement owns onboarding and the manager steps in at day 90 when a rep is behind.
- **Ramp ends at day 90.** No reinforcement, so skills fade and methods drift.
- **Delayed customer exposure.** Reps are kept off live calls until they are "ready", which slows learning.
- **No baseline.** Ramp gains can't be shown to leadership.

### 2.7 Next steps for NORAM
1. **Baseline:** pull current time to first meeting, first opp and first deal by role from Salesforce (last 4–6 cohorts).
2. **Validate the 30/60/90 tables above** with each NORAM commercial leader and agree gates and targets.
3. **Build manager ramp kits:** a weekly 1:1 agenda, call-observation scorecards and gate sign-off forms.
4. **Set up certifications:** pitch, DEPTH discovery, Payments Academy quiz, with rubrics aligned to Global.
5. **Schedule reinforcement:** automated spaced quizzes and nudges (Slack and LMS) at day 2, 7, 21 and 45 after each core module.
6. **Pilot with the next cohort,** then run a 90-day retro and iterate.

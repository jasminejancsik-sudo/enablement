# Check In: NORAM BDR Onboarding Programme
*Checkout.com · NORAM Revenue Enablement · Programme design v2*

## Summary
Check In is a five-week onboarding programme that takes early-career BDRs with little or no payments knowledge to certified and ready to prospect. Each week builds on the one before and is named after a stage of a payment. New joiners finish by passing three certifications (social selling, cold calling and pitch deck) and graduate at **Check Out**, ready to start live prospecting the following day.

The programme runs from New York and reaches all three NORAM hubs (New York, Atlanta and San Francisco) in one cohort. Every new joiner gets a **Check In Passport**: their guide to the five weeks, and the record of every deliverable they complete.

**We are asking leadership to:** confirm the BDR expectations and the proposed targets in this document, and support the programme with presenters from Product, Product Marketing, Risk, Marketing and the AE team.

## Why Check In
- **Our new BDRs start from zero on payments.** Early-career hires need payments explained from first principles before product or pitch training makes sense.
- **Onboarding today varies by manager and hub.** Check In gives every NORAM BDR the same content, the same standard and the same certifications.
- **Certification protects our brand and pipeline.** New joiners prove they meet the standard before they speak to real prospects.
- **One cohort builds a NORAM BDR team, not three hub teams.** New joiners in New York, Atlanta and San Francisco learn together and build relationships that last beyond onboarding.

## Programme at a glance
| Week | Name | The question it answers | Key outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Connect** | Who are we, and what is my role? | Set up, connected to the team and clear on what a BDR does and how they are measured |
| 2 | **Authorise** | How do payments work, and how do I work? | Can explain a payment from start to finish, describe our platform and plan a BDR week |
| 3 | **Capture** | Why would a merchant choose us? | Can qualify a merchant, position us against competitors and hand a meeting to an AE |
| 4 | **Settle** | Can I do the job? | Has run a full BDR day in simulation and rehearsed every certification |
| 5 | **Checkout** | Am I ready? | Certified in social selling, cold calling and pitch deck, and graduated |

## How the programme runs

### Schedule and time zones
- **Day 1 is a Tuesday.** The People team runs Day 1: laptops, system access, benefits and policies.
- **Office days are Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday.** These days are for workshops, role plays, simulations and certifications. Monday and Friday are virtual.
- **Live sessions run 12:00 to 4:00pm ET (9:00am to 1:00pm PT)** so all three hubs can join together.
- **Outside live hours, new joiners complete Passport work:** pre-reading, self-paced modules, shadowing and practice. In New York and Atlanta this is 9:00am to 12:00pm ET. In San Francisco it is 1:00 to 5:00pm PT.

### Daily structure (live window)
| Time (ET) | Time (PT) | Block |
|---|---|---|
| 12:00 to 12:15 | 9:00 to 9:15 | Cohort stand-up: yesterday's learning and today's goals |
| 12:15 to 1:30 | 9:15 to 10:30 | Session 1 |
| 1:45 to 3:00 | 10:45 to 12:00 | Session 2 |
| 3:00 to 4:00 | 12:00 to 1:00 | Session 3: practice, application or checkpoint |

### Hubs and formats
- **New York** is the delivery hub. Jasmine Jancsik (NORAM Enablement) and Stephanie Nelson (NORAM BDR team lead) lead sessions in the room.
- **Atlanta and San Francisco** join on video from their hub office on office days, with a **hub host** (a senior BDR or AE) in the room to run role plays and support new joiners.
- **Role plays use mixed-hub breakout rooms,** so new joiners practise with people from other hubs.
- **Format codes:** *Hybrid* = New York in the room, Atlanta and San Francisco on video from their hub. *Virtual* = everyone remote. *Self-paced* = Passport work outside live hours.

### The Check In Passport
Each new joiner receives a Passport on Day 1. It holds the week-by-week plan, the learning objectives, a starter payments glossary and a stamp page for each week. Each deliverable is a **stamp**, signed off by the named reviewer. A new joiner graduates when every stamp is signed off and all three certifications are passed.

**A BDR day (starting guidance, to be checked against our call data):**
- **Before calling:** 30 to 45 minutes of account research and planning.
- **Call block 1:** the prospect's morning, usually 8:00 to 10:00am in their time zone.
- **Midday:** emails, LinkedIn activity and Salesforce updates.
- **Call block 2:** the prospect's late afternoon, usually 4:00 to 5:30pm in their time zone.
- **Strongest calling days:** Tuesday to Thursday. Use Monday and Friday for research, sequences and social selling.

**What good looks like at the end of Week 2:** the new joiner can draw the payment flow on a whiteboard and explain each step in plain language. Their calendar for next week has two protected call blocks every day.

**Passport stamps:**
- Payment flow teach-back: draw and explain a payment from start to finish (signed off by SE or Enablement)
- Personal glossary of at least 20 payments terms
- Salesforce exercise completed (signed off by Sales Ops)
- Ideal BDR week built in their calendar (signed off by the manager)
- First LinkedIn post published

## Certifications
Certifications are taken in the order a BDR uses the skills: social selling first (finding and warming up prospects), then cold calling (opening the conversation), then the pitch deck (explaining our value). Each one is scored against a written rubric that new joiners receive in Week 2.

| Certification | What it tests | How it works | Assessed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Social selling** | A professional LinkedIn profile, a researched outreach sequence for three target contacts and one published post | Portfolio submitted on Friday of Week 4, then a 10-minute live walk-through | Enablement, Marketing |
| **2. Cold calling** | The opening, earning permission to talk, discovery using DEPTH, handling objections and booking the meeting | 20-minute live role play with a buyer persona | Stephanie Nelson, Enablement |
| **3. Pitch deck** | Explaining Checkout.com's value for one industry, linking it to the merchant's problem and answering questions | 10-minute pitch and 5-minute Q&A | Panel: Stephanie Nelson and an AE |

**Pass mark:** 80% on each rubric (*proposed*). Anyone who doesn't pass gets coaching and retakes on Thursday of Week 5. Anyone who doesn't pass a retake gets a personal coaching plan and a new date within two weeks.

## What good looks like at Check Out
A Check In graduate:
- Explains how a payment works and what Checkout.com does in plain language
- Knows our ICP and won't book a merchant we can't take on
- Runs a structured day with two protected call blocks
- Opens a cold call with confidence and uses DEPTH to run discovery
- Uses LinkedIn to research, connect and stay visible to prospects
- Hands every meeting to their AE with a complete handoff note
- Keeps Salesforce up to date the same day
